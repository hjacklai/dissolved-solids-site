Hari Raya Aidiladha in PJ.

A respectful non-alcoholic cocktail-bar plan for Aidiladha in Petaling Jaya. Our NA programme, the coffee and tea side, and why the small-bar format works for observing guests.

Hari Raya Aidiladha (the Feast of Sacrifice) marks the close of the Hajj. For Muslim guests across Malaysia, the day is observed with prayer, qurban, and family meals. The cocktail bar, on this day, runs entirely on the non-alcoholic side. The same craft, the same glassware, the same care. Just no alcohol.

What we mean by "NA-focused" on Aidiladha

A serious NA cocktail menu at a craft bar is a real menu. The bartender builds the drink with the same technique: shake, stir, dilute, strain, garnish, present. The only thing that changes is the spirit base.

On Aidiladha, our NA programme is the default for guests who specify they are observing. The alcoholic menu remains available for guests who are not. The glassware is identical on both sides; the drinks land at the table at the same time.

What to order on Aidiladha

The Aidiladha line-up draws on Malaysian and Middle Eastern flavours, both reflecting the day's heritage.

Rose and Cardamom Cooler: rose syrup, cardamom, lime, soda. Floral and warm-spiced.

Date and Tamarind Sour: date syrup, tamarind, lemon, ice. Sweet-tart, deeply Middle Eastern.

Saffron and Honey Lemonade: saffron-infused syrup, honey, lemon, soda.

NA Bandung: rose syrup, evaporated milk, ice. The Malaysian classic, in a wine glass.

Sirap Selasih: rose syrup with basil seeds. Traditional Malaysian, beloved by older guests.

Pandan and Coconut Refresher: pandan syrup, coconut water, lime, soda. Malaysian-local, entirely NA.

Iced Karak Chai: proper karak chai, cooled, with cardamom and saffron.

Kopi-O Peng in a Coupe: cold-brewed kopi-O with gula melaka, served as a cocktail.

Coffee and tea on Aidiladha

The coffee and tea programme runs alongside the NA cocktails:

  • Cold-brewed kopi-O with gula melaka, in a coupe
  • Karak chai (cardamom-led milk tea), iced or hot
  • Yemeni-style coffee with cardamom
  • Single-origin pour-overs
  • Brewed jasmine tea, hot or iced

The Aidiladha evening plan

Morning, prayers and qurban: the actual observance.

Lunch with family, 1pm-4pm: the rendang, the lemang, the kuih.

Afternoon visits, 4pm-7pm: open-house rounds across family and friends.

Evening at the bar, 8pm-10pm: a quiet sit. NA cocktails, coffee, tea. The cocktail bar is for after the family rounds, when you want a quiet room with a friend.

For groups mixing observers and non-observers

The bar handles both. The bartender pours the NA cocktail for one guest and the alcoholic version for another, in matching glassware, at the same time. We do not announce which is which.

How we run the day

We stay open on Aidiladha (regular evening hours). The room is quieter than a normal Friday or Saturday. Walk-ins generally work. For groups of 4 or more, WhatsApp ahead.
