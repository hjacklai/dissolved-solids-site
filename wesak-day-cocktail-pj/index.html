Wesak Day in PJ.

Wesak Day in Petaling Jaya. The Buddhist holiday tends towards sobriety and quiet observance. Our non-alcoholic side is real, the bar is quiet on this public holiday, and how a respectful afternoon visit can fit.

Wesak Day commemorates the birth, enlightenment, and passing of the Buddha. For Malaysian Buddhists, it is a day of temple visits, almsgiving, observance, and quiet reflection. The traditional observance does not include alcohol. This page is for everyone who wants to mark Wesak Day with restraint, whether you are observant or simply respect the spirit of the day.

The Wesak Day approach

Wesak Day is not the right occasion for a celebration-format cocktail evening. We acknowledge that openly. What we can offer:

  • A real non-alcoholic afternoon programme. Proper loose-leaf tea, fresh-pulled espresso, coffee mocktails, NA spritzes, kombucha when the brew is ready.
  • A quiet small room. Both outlets are 30-seat venues. Wesak Day afternoon is one of the quietest hours of the year.
  • No theming of any kind. We do not run "Buddhist-themed cocktails" or anything that would feel performative.
  • Outside food welcome. If you are bringing vegetarian food from a temple-adjacent restaurant, we will serve glasses of water and small plates if you ask.

What to order on Wesak Day

The non-alcoholic side at our outlets is properly built. For Wesak Day specifically, the tea programme is the most fitting:

Chinese tea programme:

  • Pu-erh (cooked or aged): the traditional Buddhist-monastery tea. Earthy, deeply meditative.
  • Tieguanyin oolong: floral-vegetal, ceremonial in feel.
  • Chrysanthemum: light, cleansing, the cooling tea Malaysian Chinese families drink with vegetarian meals.
  • Jasmine pearl: floral, delicate.
  • Pure green tea (longjing or sencha when available): grassy, slightly sweet.

Served in proper cups with hot water for re-steeping. Multiple infusions from the same leaves.

Non-alcoholic builds:

  • Chrysanthemum honey cooler: chrysanthemum tea, honey, lemon, ice.
  • NA roselle: hibiscus syrup, lime, soda. Deep red without alcohol.
  • NA pandan refresher: pandan syrup, lime, coconut water, soda.
  • Coffee mocktails: Black Honey, Floral Mango. Espresso-led, no alcohol.

Proper coffee: espresso, AeroPress manual brews, kopi-O. Each pulled or brewed to order.

The vegetarian-food question

Many Malaysian Buddhists eat vegetarian on Wesak Day (some all day, some for the main meal). Outside food is welcome at both our outlets. If you have brought vegetarian food from a temple kitchen or a vegetarian restaurant, you can pair it with our tea or NA drinks in the bar.

For groups planning a small Wesak Day gathering at the bar with brought-in vegetarian food, WhatsApp ahead and we will reserve the table seats (rather than the bar counter) so there is room for the food.

Why we do not run a Wesak Day cocktail programme

Wesak is a day of restraint and reflection. Promoting an alcoholic cocktail evening on this date would be tone-deaf at best. We acknowledge the day by leaning into the NA programme and the tea programme, and by accepting that the bar is quieter than usual. Some of our regulars who are not Buddhist also choose Wesak Day for a sober afternoon visit; that fits the spirit of the day.

If you are visiting with mixed-belief friends

A group with one or two observant Buddhists plus other friends works well at our outlets. The NA programme means the observant Buddhists are not stuck with "the lime juice option". Non-Buddhist friends can have a quiet drink without the room being a party. Tell the bartender when you arrive; the round comes out balanced.
