Pandan Collins

A Malaysian Tom Collins. Gin, fresh pandan syrup, lime, soda. Built tall on plenty of ice. Best in the late afternoon, when the light is going soft.

Served at Soluble Solids · SS2 Petaling Jaya

Ingredients

  • Gin (a juniper-led London dry works best)
  • Fresh pandan syrup
  • Fresh lime juice
  • Soda water to top
  • Pandan leaf knot and dehydrated lime to garnish

Method

  1. Combine gin, pandan syrup, and lime juice in a shaker over plenty of ice.
  2. Shake hard for 8 to 10 seconds. Enough to chill and dilute, not foam.
  3. Strain into a chilled tall glass over fresh ice.
  4. Top with cold soda water, gently.
  5. Tie a pandan leaf into a knot and slip it down the inside of the glass. Float a dehydrated lime wheel.

Measurements are a guide. Our bartenders adjust ratios at service for the best balance. Natural ingredients (citrus, herbs, syrups, fresh fruit) shift in intensity through the season.

How it tastes

Pandan up front, lime mid-palate, gin underneath holding it all together. The juniper and the pandan trade off in interesting ways. Pandan reads as grassy and slightly vanilla on its own, but with juniper it gets sharper and more aromatic. The lime is doing structural work more than flavour work. Soda for length. This is a long, slow drink rather than a hit.

Why we built it

The Tom Collins is one of the easiest cocktails in the world to make and one of the easiest to overlook. Swapping the sugar syrup for pandan syrup turns a familiar template into something that tastes specifically Malaysian without trying too hard. We're not the first bar to do this. We've just kept tightening the proportions until the pandan reads clearly without anyone having to ask what it is. More on pandan extraction methods here.

Frequently asked questions

What glass is the Pandan Collins served in?

A tall Collins glass over fresh ice, with a knotted pandan leaf tucked down the inside of the glass and a dehydrated lime wheel floated on top. The Collins glass holds the volume of a long drink and the pandan knot extends extra aroma into the glass as you drink.

Can I substitute the pandan syrup?

Pandan (the leaf of Pandanus amaryllifolius) is the structural aromatic and not really substitutable. The grassy-vanilla character is what makes this a Pandan Collins and not just a Tom Collins. Vanilla syrup is the closest western substitute and gives you a vanilla Tom Collins (also nice, different drink). Fresh pandan leaves are readily available at Malaysian wet markets at RM 3 a bunch; just make the syrup.

How strong is the Pandan Collins?

Around 10 to 13 percent ABV in the glass after the soda top and ice melt. The 45ml of gin is the alcohol load; the pandan syrup, lime, and roughly 90ml of soda add volume but no extra spirit. Long format, low strength, built for hot afternoons or as the second drink of the night. Easy to pace.

What food pairs with the Pandan Collins?

Anything Southeast Asian and herbal. Otak-otak. Nasi lemak with sambal. Vietnamese fresh spring rolls. Lemongrass-chicken skewers. For dessert pairings, pandan chiffon cake or kuih seri muka (the pandan in the drink picks up the pandan on the plate). Light fried snacks like fried tofu or sotong goreng tepung. Avoid heavy curries; the drink is built to refresh.
