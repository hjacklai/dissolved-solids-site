Espresso Martini

Vodka, fresh espresso, espresso liqueur. Shaken hard for the foam. The most-ordered coffee cocktail in the world, made with care because the easy ones are the easiest to ruin.

Served at Dissolved Solids · Damansara Kim

Ingredients

  • Vodka, a clean grain vodka
  • Fresh espresso, just-pulled, slightly cooled
  • Espresso liqueur
  • Demerara or vanilla syrup
  • Three coffee beans to garnish

Method

  1. Pull the espresso to order. Fresher shot, better crema.
  2. Combine vodka, espresso, espresso liqueur, and syrup in a shaker over ice.
  3. Shake hard for 12 to 15 seconds. Long enough to chill, dilute, and build foam.
  4. Double-strain into a chilled coupe.
  5. Float three coffee beans in a small triangle.

Measurements are a guide. Our bartenders adjust ratios at service for the best balance. Natural ingredients (citrus, herbs, syrups, fresh fruit) shift in intensity through the season.

How it tastes

Bitter coffee on first sip, vodka in the body, sweetness on the finish. The whole drink should be cold, dense, and slightly bitter, with a foam that lasts most of the way through. People often blame a bad espresso martini on the espresso. Usually it's the dilution. Under-shaken means warm and weak. Over-shaken means watery. Twelve to fifteen seconds with hard, fast shaking is the window.

Why we built it

The Espresso Martini was invented in 1980s London by Dick Bradsell, allegedly for a model who asked for "something that'll wake me up and then mess me up." It's been on every cocktail menu in the world since the 2010s revival. We make ours with a slight twist when we feel like it, swapping the espresso for cold kopi-O concentrate. The robusta carries through the shake better than arabica espresso does. More on robusta in cocktails here.
