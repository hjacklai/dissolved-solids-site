The Bleggnogg

Espresso, whole egg, cane sugar. Shaken hard, served cold, dusted with nutmeg. An eggnog shaped like a coffee. Has no spirit, no dairy, no apology.

Served at Dissolved Solids · Damansara Kim

Ingredients

  • Fresh espresso, double shot, slightly cooled
  • 1 whole fresh egg
  • Pure cane sugar syrup
  • Light dusting of grated nutmeg
  • Three coffee beans to garnish
  • (zero alcohol)

Method

  1. Crack the whole egg into a shaker. Add espresso and cane sugar syrup.
  2. Dry shake hard for 10 seconds. This emulsifies the egg into the coffee.
  3. Add ice and shake again for 12 to 15 seconds, until very cold.
  4. Double-strain into a chilled coupe.
  5. Dust the foam with nutmeg and float three coffee beans.

Measurements are a guide. Our bartenders adjust ratios at service for the best balance. Natural ingredients (citrus, herbs, syrups, fresh fruit) shift in intensity through the season.

How it tastes

Coffee first, eggnog second, sugar third. The whole egg gives the drink a thick, almost custardy mouthfeel that ordinary coffee drinks can't reach. The egg yolk also rounds the espresso's bitter edge. Without dairy, the coffee stays sharper than a latte but softer than a black espresso. The nutmeg on top is the eggnog signature. People who haven't met a proper egg-cocktail before are sometimes wary. After one sip, the wariness usually goes away.

Why we built it

Bleggnogg is one of our oldest house drinks. The name is a portmanteau of "black" + "eggnog" that an early customer coined and that we couldn't bring ourselves to drop. We make it because eggnog is a beautiful texture profile that almost no bar uses outside December, and because pairing it with espresso (which already has its own foam culture) made the texture even better. It's also the easiest answer to "do you have anything like a coffee dessert that isn't sweet?" More on our non-alcoholic philosophy here.

Frequently asked questions

What glass is the Bleggnogg served in?

A chilled coupe, double-strained. The shallow bowl shows the thick beige foam and the three floating coffee beans, which are the drink's visual signatures. A rocks glass also works but loses the foam display; the dry shake builds height that the coupe shows off best.

Can I substitute the whole egg?

Aquafaba (chickpea brine) works for vegan versions but loses the custard mouthfeel that defines the drink. Pasteurised egg whites work for safety but lose the yolk's richness that rounds the espresso's bitter edge. The whole egg is structural. If you want it without egg, drink our Espresso Martini or Floral Mango instead.

How strong is the Bleggnogg?

Zero alcohol. The drink contains no spirit. Caffeine is the only stimulant (about 80 to 120mg from the double espresso). Safe to drive after; bad idea right before bed. The whole egg is the body, the espresso is the engine, the nutmeg is the eggnog signature.

What food pairs with the Bleggnogg?

Brunch-shaped food and desserts. Croissants, banana bread, chocolate cake, tiramisu, custard tart, salted caramel pudding. The egg-and-coffee profile also bridges into savoury brunch (eggs Benedict, smoked salmon bagel). Avoid spicy heat; the custard mouthfeel doesn't want competition.
