Kopi Sour

A Malaysian coffee whisky sour. Cold kopi-O, whisky or aged rum, gula melaka, lemon, foamed. Bitter, sweet, sharp, dense. The drink we use to convince people that coffee belongs in cocktails.

Served at Dissolved Solids · Damansara Kim

Ingredients

  • Whisky (a soft bourbon) or aged rum
  • Cold kopi-O, strong (sock-brewed if possible)
  • Fresh lemon juice
  • Gula melaka or dark palm sugar syrup
  • 1 small egg white, or 20ml aquafaba
  • Three drops aromatic bitters on the foam

Method

  1. Combine spirit, kopi-O, lemon, gula melaka syrup, and egg white in a shaker.
  2. Dry shake (no ice) for 8 seconds to build the foam.
  3. Add ice and shake hard for 12 to 15 seconds.
  4. Double-strain into a chilled coupe or sour glass.
  5. Drop three bitters on the foam and pull a knife through them for a feathered pattern.

Measurements are a guide. Our bartenders adjust ratios at service for the best balance. Natural ingredients (citrus, herbs, syrups, fresh fruit) shift in intensity through the season.

How it tastes

The kopi-O is the loudest ingredient and the foam is the quietest. The whisky shows up in the middle, the lemon sharpens the edge, and the gula melaka catches everything on the way down. People expect a kopi sour to taste like cold brew with booze. It doesn't. The acid completely changes how the coffee reads. It's sharper, more focused, less heavy. The bitters on top add a fragrant nose that the coffee doesn't have on its own.

Why we built it

The whisky sour is one of the most forgiving cocktail templates ever written. Swap the sugar for gula melaka and add cold kopi-O and you've got something that feels specifically Malaysian without losing the structure that makes the original work. Robusta is the right coffee here. Arabica disappears under the spirit. More on why we use robusta and what kopi-O actually is.
