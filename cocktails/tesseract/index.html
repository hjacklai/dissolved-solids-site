The Tesseract.

Our signature cocktail. Simple presentation, four flavour dimensions: smoky, fruity, savoury, velvet. A drink built for guests who seek unique flavours and a progressive drinking experience as the cocktail dilutes in the glass.

Served at Dissolved Solids · Damansara Kim·House creation

Ingredients

  • Gin (London Dry, juniper-led)
  • Mezcal (espadín, a small measure)
  • Maraschino liqueur (Luxardo)
  • Cointreau
  • Saline solution, 2 to 3 drops to balance
  • Citric acid solution, 2 to 3 drops to balance
  • A splash of tonic to finish

Method

  1. Combine the gin, mezcal, maraschino, and Cointreau in a chilled mixing glass.
  2. Add the saline and the citric acid solutions, 2 to 3 drops each.
  3. Add ice and stir long and cold. The saline and citric need time to fully integrate; rushing the stir leaves edges in the drink.
  4. Strain up into a frozen coupe.
  5. Finish with a small splash of tonic on the surface. The quinine and the carbonation lift the aromatics on the first sip.

The Tesseract is built by feel at service. Drop counts adjust to the day's ingredients and the room's temperature. Our bartenders calibrate at the bar.

Why we called it the Tesseract

A tesseract is the four-dimensional analogue of a cube. A square is two dimensions; a cube is three; a tesseract is four. You cannot draw a tesseract on paper without compromise, and you cannot fully picture it in your head; the projection onto a 2D page is a hint at something more.

The cocktail is named for that idea. The presentation is simple, just a small coupe with a clear-amber drink in it, no theatre, no garnish-heavy reveal. Underneath, the flavour has four dimensions. Each one reads on a different part of the tongue, at a different part of the sip, and the geometry shifts as the drink dilutes.

The four flavour dimensions

Smoky. The mezcal carries the smoke, but only just. The pour is small enough that you do not taste smoke on the front of the palate; you notice it as a low note running underneath the gin. The espadín adds a mineral, almost desert-rock quality that gin alone does not have.

Fruity. The maraschino brings a high cherry note. Cherry without sweetness, because the maraschino is dry; cherry as a structural element, not a dessert flavour. Cointreau supports the fruit with bitter orange, which threads through the middle of the sip.

Savoury. The saline solution does the work. 2 to 3 drops of 20% salt water is enough to round all the edges in the drink. The saline pulls the bitter and the sweet together and gives the cocktail a savoury depth that most stirred drinks do not have.

Velvet. The citric acid balance and the long stir produce a texture more than a flavour. The drink lands silky on the tongue, almost viscous on the second sip, and that mouthfeel is where the cocktail starts to feel like more than the sum of its parts.

None of the dimensions reads as dominant. The Tesseract is not a "smoky cocktail" or a "fruity cocktail" or a "savoury cocktail". It is all four at once, geometrically overlapping.

The progressive experience

Most cocktails taste the strongest on the first sip and lose definition as they dilute. The Tesseract is built differently. Drink it in three phases:

First sip. Cold, dense, slightly closed. The tonic splash you smell first. The gin and the maraschino lead. The mezcal is barely there; the saline is invisible.

Middle of the glass. The ice has melted slightly, the drink has opened up. The mezcal emerges as a low note. The Cointreau orange threads through. The saline starts pulling the flavours together. The drink feels denser and more rounded than at the start.

End of the glass. The dilution has the citric acid showing on the finish. The texture peaks. The four dimensions are all on the palate at the same time. This is the part of the drink that justifies the name.

The Tesseract is not a drink to gulp. It is a drink to follow through three sips and watch how it changes. Most guests order another after the first one because the experience is not finished when the glass is empty.

What we built it for

The Tesseract is on the menu for guests who tell the bartender "give me something I have not had before". It is the answer to that question. The flavour profile is distinct from the canonical short stirred drinks; you cannot place it as a Negroni variant or a Martini variant or a Manhattan variant. It works on its own terms.

If you order one, expect a brief explanation from the bartender. The Tesseract is one of the few drinks we explain at service, because the four-dimensions framing is part of the experience. We are not selling theatre; we are pointing out what to look for so the drink does not pass you by.

The build details that matter

Three things make a Tesseract land properly:

The mezcal ratio. A small measure, no more. Espadín that runs vegetal rather than aggressively smoky. The drink is not a margarita variant.

The drop counts. 2 to 3 drops each of saline and citric. Over-pour either and you tip the balance. Under-pour and the drink reads as a flat stirred mix.

The stir. Long enough that the saline and citric integrate completely. Short stir and you taste them as separate elements. Our bartenders stir the Tesseract longer than they stir a Negroni.

The tonic splash on top is the only theatre. It opens the aromatics so the first inhale is the most layered moment of the drink.

How to order it

Walk in or reserve. Ask for the Tesseract by name. The bartender will check whether you want the brief explanation at service or whether you would rather discover the four dimensions on your own. Both work.
