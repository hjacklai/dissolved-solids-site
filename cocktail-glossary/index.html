Cocktail glossary.

A working A-Z of cocktail and bartending terms. The technique words, the ingredient categories, the format names, the Malaysian-local additions. Plain English, no jargon, no fluff.

300+ terms·Updated May 2026
ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPRSTVWXYZ

A

Absinthe
High-proof anise-based spirit. Used as a rinse in classics like the Sazerac and Corpse Reviver No. 2.
Acid adjustment
Bartender technique using citric, malic, tartaric, or phosphoric acid to control sourness independently of citrus juice. See acid adjustment.
Agave syrup
Sweetener derived from the agave plant. Pairs naturally with tequila and mezcal.
Amaro
Italian bitter herbal liqueur. Campari and Aperol are aperitivo amari; Fernet-Branca and Ramazzotti are digestivo amari. See amaro explained.
Angostura bitters
The default aromatic bitter. From Trinidad. In almost every classic cocktail recipe.
Aperitivo / Aperitif
Low-alcohol drink served before a meal to stimulate appetite. Aperol Spritz, Negroni, Americano are aperitivos.
Aquafaba
The water from a can of chickpeas. Vegan substitute for egg white in foamed cocktails.
Asam boi
Malaysian salty-sour preserved plum. Modern cocktail ingredient. See asam boi in cocktails.
Aging (spirits)
Resting a spirit in oak barrels to develop flavour, colour, and texture.
Amaretto
Italian almond-flavoured liqueur. Used in Amaretto Sour, French Connection.
Añejo
Tequila aged 1-3 years in oak. Darker, smoother, often sipped neat.
Aperol
Italian aperitif, bitter-sweet orange-rhubarb. Base of the Aperol Spritz.
Aromatised wine
Wine infused with botanicals (vermouth, Lillet, Cocchi Americano).
Arrack
South Asian / Sri Lankan coconut-flower distillate. Funky, used in Tiki and modern punch.
Atomiser
Spray bottle used to mist a small amount of spirit (absinthe, perfumed bitters) over a finished drink.
Agar clarification
Technique using agar (seaweed gel) to clarify cocktails. Less common than milk-washing but works for vegan builds.
Aging by oak
Storing a finished cocktail in oak (small barrel) for weeks to months. Develops oak character, integrates flavours.
Akvavit / Aquavit
Scandinavian caraway-led grain spirit. Cocktail modifier with a savoury edge.
Apple brandy
Calvados (French) or applejack (American). Distilled from apple cider. Used in Jack Rose.
Acidified water
Water with citric or malic acid added. Used to brighten low-acid juices or as a flavour modulator.

B

Bar spoon
Long, twisted-handle spoon used for stirring and measuring. One barspoon = 5ml.
Bénédictine
French herbal liqueur. Component of the Vieux Carré and Singapore Sling.
Bianco vermouth
White sweet vermouth. Lighter and more floral than rosso (sweet red) vermouth.
Bitter (cocktail family)
Drinks built around a bittering agent (Campari, amaro, etc). Negroni, Boulevardier, Americano. See the Negroni family tree.
Bitters
Concentrated flavour extracts used in small doses to season cocktails. Angostura, Peychaud's, orange. See bitters explained.
Bottled cocktail
Cocktail pre-batched, bottled, and served by pouring. Increasingly common at high-volume bars.
Build
To assemble a cocktail directly in the serving glass, no shaker. Mojito, G&T, Old Fashioned can all be built.
Bunga kantan
Malay name for torch ginger flower. Used in cocktails for floral-vegetal accents. See bunga kantan in cocktails.
Bunga telang
Malay name for butterfly pea flower. Colour-changing cocktail ingredient. See butterfly pea flower.
Bitter lemon
Carbonated mixer flavoured with quinine and lemon. Bitterer than tonic.
Blanco (tequila)
Unaged tequila. Crisp, agave-forward. The standard Margarita base.
Boston shaker
Two-piece shaker (mixing tin + pint glass, or two tins). Pro-bartender standard.
Bottled cocktail
Cocktail pre-batched, bottled, and served by pouring. Increasingly common at high-volume bars.
Bourbon
American whiskey, minimum 51% corn mash, new charred oak. Sweeter than rye.
Brut
Driest classification for sparkling wine (0-12g/L sugar).
Build (technique)
Assembling a cocktail directly in the serving glass without a shaker. Old Fashioned, Mojito, G&T are built.
Butterfly pea
See Bunga telang.
Bain-marie
Hot water bath used to gently warm cocktail components without scorching (caramelising sugar, melting honey).
Balance
The most-used cocktail evaluation word. A balanced cocktail has no dominant note; spirit, citrus, sweet, and bitter all read clearly.
Bandung
Malaysian rose-syrup-and-milk drink. Cocktail variants substitute gin or vodka.
Bartender's choice
Asking the bartender to build something based on description. Common at small craft bars.
Beepit
Malaysian POS system used at Dissolved Solids for online ordering and reservations.
Big ice
Large-format ice (king cubes, spheres). Slower-melting than standard cubes.
Blend (frozen)
Mixing cocktail components with crushed ice in a blender. Pina Colada, Frozen Margarita, Daiquiri Frozen.

C

Calamansi
Malaysian small green-orange citrus. Limau kasturi. Tastes between lime and orange. See calamansi.
Campari
Bright-red Italian bitter aperitif. Defining ingredient of the Negroni.
Cili padi
Malaysian bird's-eye chilli. Used in cocktails for heat. See cili padi in cocktails.
Clarification / Clarified cocktail
Technique using milk or agar to remove cloudiness, producing crystal-clear cocktails. See milk-washing.
Cocchi Americano
Italian quinine-based aperitif wine. Substitute for Lillet Blanc in modern recipes.
Cointreau
French triple-sec orange liqueur. Component of the Margarita, Cosmopolitan, Sidecar.
Cold brew
Coffee or tea extracted in cold water over hours rather than minutes. Lower in acid, fuller in body. See cold brew cocktails.
Crème de cacao
Chocolate liqueur. White (clear) or dark. Used in Alexanders and Grasshoppers.
Crème de mûre
Blackberry liqueur. Used in the Bramble.
Crème de violette
Violet liqueur. Used in the Aviation.
Curaçao
Orange liqueur from the Dutch Caribbean. Component of the Mai Tai, Sidecar.
Cachaça
Brazilian sugarcane spirit. Base of the Caipirinha.
Calvados
French apple (and pear) brandy from Normandy.
Carbonation
Adding CO2 to a liquid. Soda streams or kegged systems work for cocktails. See carbonation at home.
Chartreuse
French herbal liqueur made by Carthusian monks. Green (110 proof) and Yellow (80 proof).
Cherry liqueur
Cherry Heering or maraschino. Component of Blood and Sand, Singapore Sling.
Citric acid
The acid in lemon and lime. Used in powdered form for acid adjustment.
Cobbler shaker
Three-piece shaker with built-in strainer in the cap. Home-bartender standard.
Cognac
French grape brandy from Cognac region. Classifications: VS, VSOP, XO.
Cordial
Sweetened concentrate, often citrus-based (lime cordial, elderflower cordial).
Coupe
Shallow stemmed cocktail glass. Standard for "up" pours like Daiquiris, sours.
Cream (heavy)
Double cream / whipping cream. Used in dessert and creamy cocktails (Brandy Alexander, White Russian).
Citrus oils
The aromatic compounds in citrus peel. Released by twisting or expressing the peel.
Carbonation level
Measured in CO2 volumes. Tonic is around 4, soda water around 4-5, sparkling wine 5-6.
Clamp / Hawthorne
The spring-strainer that fits over a Boston shaker tin.
Coconut sugar
Gula kelapa. The coconut-palm cousin of gula melaka. Similar caramel profile.
Centrifuge
Lab equipment used by modern bartenders to clarify cocktails by spinning out solids. Expensive; the alternative is milk-wash or agar.
Cocktail set
The complete tool kit: shaker, jigger, bar spoon, strainer, muddler, mixing glass.
Crushed ice ratio
Crushed ice melts 3-4x faster than cubes. Drinks with crushed ice (Mint Julep, Swizzle, Tiki) are calibrated for that fast dilution.

D

Dash
Small amount of bitters or flavour liquid. Roughly 0.5-1ml from a standard dasher bottle.
Demerara syrup
Syrup made from demerara (unrefined) sugar. Adds molasses notes; deeper than simple syrup.
Double strain
Strain through both a Hawthorne strainer (on the shaker) and a fine mesh strainer (over the glass). Removes ice shards and pulp.
Dry shake
Shaking egg-white-containing cocktails first without ice, to emulsify the protein, then again with ice.
Dry vermouth
Crisp white vermouth. Component of the Martini.
Daiquiri (template)
Spirit + lime + sugar. The cleanest sour template.
Dasher
The small-hole stopper on a bitters bottle that releases one "dash" at a time.
Demerara rum
Heavy, dark Guyanese rum. Component of tiki classics.
Distillation
The process of separating ethanol from fermented liquid via heat and condensation.
Drambuie
Scotch whisky liqueur with honey and herbs. Component of the Rusty Nail.
Dilution by surface area
Crushed ice has more surface area than a single big cube; melts faster; dilutes faster.
Daiquiri (frozen)
Blended-with-ice Daiquiri. Cuban café staple. Hemingway's preferred drink at El Floridita.
Density (pour layering)
Liquid density determines layer order in pousse-café. Denser liquids sink; lighter liquids float.
Direct method
Building a cocktail directly in the serving glass without a shaker (vs mixing-glass method). Highballs, Old Fashioneds.
Durian
Malaysian king of fruits. Bold cocktail ingredient. See durian.

E

Egg white
Used to create silky foam in sours. Dry shake first. See why egg white in cocktails.
Elderflower liqueur
St-Germain is the brand. Floral, easy to use, in many modern cocktails.
Espresso
Pulled-shot Italian coffee. Component of Espresso Martini and modern coffee cocktails. See espresso in cocktails.
Expressed peel
Citrus peel oils sprayed over a drink by twisting the peel sharply. Aromatic finishing touch.
Elderflower cordial
Non-alcoholic floral concentrate. The base of NA Hugo Spritz.
Espadín
The most common agave variety used for mezcal. The "house" mezcal style.
Ethyl alcohol
The intoxicating component of all alcoholic drinks. Also called ethanol.
Extract
Concentrated flavour solution in alcohol. Vanilla extract, almond extract, etc.
Egg yolk
Used in dessert cocktails (Eggnog) and a few flips. Creates a rich, custardy texture.
Emulsion
The state of egg-white sour foam: protein and air locked into stable bubbles by shaking.
Eau-de-vie
French clear fruit brandy. Pear (poire Williams), raspberry (framboise), kirsch (cherry).

F

Falernum
Spiced syrup from Barbados. Lime, clove, almond, ginger. Tiki ingredient.
Fat-wash
Technique infusing spirits with fat (bacon-bourbon, coconut-rum). See fat-washing spirits.
Fermentation
Microbial conversion of sugar to alcohol (yeast) or acid (lactic-acid bacteria). See our journal for fermentation pieces.
Fernet-Branca
Intensely bitter Italian amaro. Component of the Hanky Panky.
Fix
Vintage cocktail format: spirit + lemon + sugar + ice + a fruit garnish.
Fizz
Sour-format cocktail topped with soda. Gin Fizz, Ramos Gin Fizz.
Fennel
Anise-aromatic seed and bulb. Cocktail spice and digestive note.
Fino sherry
Dry, pale sherry from Andalusia. Aperitivo and cocktail modifier.
Float
Layering a small amount of liquid on top of a finished cocktail. New York Sour floats red wine.
Fortified wine
Wine with added spirit (raising ABV to 15-22%). Sherry, port, vermouth.
French 75 (template)
Gin + lemon + sugar + champagne. The celebratory classic.
Fermentation (cocktail)
Pre-fermenting ingredients (tepache, shrub, kombucha) for cocktail use. Adds acid, body, complexity.
Filtration (Brita / charcoal)
Filtering tap water through activated charcoal removes chlorine for cleaner ice. Worth it for big-cube programmes.
Float (technique)
Pouring a small amount of liquid on top of a finished cocktail (red wine on New York Sour). The float sits on the surface briefly before sinking.
Frothy
The mouthfeel of egg-white or aquafaba-foamed sours.
Fruit milk-wash
Modern milk-washing technique using fruit acids to clarify. Less common than citrus-acid version.

G

Gentian
Root used as the bittering agent in many amari and homemade bitters.
Genever
Dutch malt-based gin predecessor. Maltier and richer than London Dry gin.
Glassware
Coupe, rocks, highball, flute, Nick & Nora, hurricane, tiki, julep cup. See glassware explained.
Gula melaka
Malaysian palm sugar. Deep, caramel-clove flavour. See gula melaka.
Galangal
Southeast Asian ginger-cousin. Less hot, more aromatic. See galangal.
Gimlet (template)
Gin + lime + sweetener. The Royal Navy's anti-scurvy cocktail.
Gin (London Dry)
The standard gin style. Juniper-led, dry, made by re-distillation with botanicals.
Ginger beer
Fermented or brewed ginger drink, carbonated. The mixer in Moscow Mule, Dark and Stormy.
Ginger bug
Wild-yeast starter for homemade fermented ginger drinks. See ginger bug cordials.
Green Chartreuse
The original Chartreuse, 110 proof, herbal-intense.
Grenadine
Pomegranate-based syrup. Real grenadine, not the red corn-syrup kind.
Garnish theory
The principles of cocktail garnish: aromatic, visual, functional. See garnish theory.
Genepi
Alpine herbal liqueur, similar register to Chartreuse but more austere. Used in our house Stronk cocktail.
Glass chilling
Pre-cooling the serving glass (in the freezer or with ice + soda) so the drink stays cold longer.
Gomme syrup
Sugar syrup thickened with gum arabic. Adds a silky mouthfeel. Pre-1920s classic technique.

H

Hawthorne strainer
Spring-coil strainer that fits over a shaker tin. The standard cocktail strainer.
Highball
Spirit + carbonated mixer + ice in a tall glass. G&T, Mojito, Moscow Mule.
Hibiscus / Roselle
Hibiscus sabdariffa, dried calyces used in tart-tropical cocktails. See hibiscus and roselle.
Heat (Scoville)
Heat measurement scale for chilli peppers. Cili padi sits around 50,000-100,000 SHU.
Highball glass
Tall, straight-sided glass (240-360ml). For G&T, highballs, Tom Collins.
Honey syrup
2:1 honey to warm water. Use instead of simple syrup for Bee's Knees, Gold Rush.
House signature
An original cocktail unique to a specific bar. Often the bar's calling card.
Hard shake (Japanese)
The Kazuo Uyeda technique. Three-axis high-frequency shake; produces visibly aerated drinks.
Hot Toddy (format)
Whisky + hot water + honey + lemon + clove. Winter / cold-cure classic.

I

Ice (clear)
Ice frozen using directional freezing so impurities are pushed out. Visually striking, slower-melting. See clear ice at home.
Ice (crushed)
Pebble-sized ice used for tiki and julep formats. Faster melt, more dilution.
Infusion
Steeping a flavour ingredient in a spirit, syrup, or water. The basis of customised cocktails.
Ice ball / sphere
Large single sphere of ice. Slower-melting than cubes, used for stirred drinks served in rocks glasses.
Ice in cocktails (theory)
Ice does three jobs: chill, dilute, aerate. Bigger ice slows dilution; crushed accelerates it. See ice in cocktails.
Ice spear
Long rectangular ice column cut to fit highball glasses. Slow-melting, photogenic.
Imperial measure
Older British measure: jigger = 1 oz = 28ml. Most modern bars use metric (30/15ml).

J

Jigger
Two-sided measuring cup. Standard sizes: 30ml/15ml or 45ml/22ml.
Julep
Spirit + sugar + mint + crushed ice in a metal cup. Mint Julep is the canonical version.
Japanese hard shake
The Kazuo Uyeda technique. Three-axis high-frequency shake. Produces visibly more aerated drinks.
Jiggering
The act of measuring with a jigger. Pro bartenders jigger every pour for consistency.
Jaggery
Indian unrefined cane or palm sugar. Similar to gula melaka. Used in Ponggal cocktails.
Japanese style (cocktail bar)
The precise, ritualised bartending tradition from Tokyo / Ginza. Hard shake, hand-carved ice, etc.
Junmai
Sake style with no added distilled alcohol. The standard cocktail sake.

K

Kaffir lime leaf / Makrut
Distinctive Southeast Asian citrus aromatic. See kaffir lime leaf.
Kahm yeast
White surface film on fermented vegetables. Harmless but ugly. Skim and discard.
Kombucha
Sweet-tea ferment using a SCOBY. Cocktail ingredient. See tropical kombucha.
Kopi-O
Malaysian black coffee. See Malaysian kopi explained.
King cube
Single large cube of clear ice, typically 5x5cm. Standard for Old Fashioned-format drinks.
Kelulut honey
Stingless bee honey from Sarawak. Naturally sour and runny. See kelulut honey.
Kombucha second fermentation
Adding fruit or sugar to fermented kombucha for carbonation and flavour. See kombucha second fermentation.

L

Lacto-fermentation
Salt-based fermentation producing lactic acid. See lacto-fermentation.
Limoncello
Italian lemon-peel liqueur. Used as a digestivo and as a cocktail modifier.
Long drink
Cocktail served in a tall glass with significant non-spirit volume. G&T, Tom Collins, Mojito.
Lemongrass
Southeast Asian aromatic grass. Citrus-grass syrup, gin pairing. See lemongrass.
Lillet Blanc
French aperitif wine, dry-bitter-floral. Component of the Vesper, Corpse Reviver No.2.
Lime cordial
Sweetened lime concentrate. Rose's is the original. The original Gimlet uses cordial, not fresh lime.
London Dry
The standard gin style. Juniper-led, dry, no added sugar, all flavour from re-distillation.
Lacto-fermented (fruit)
Wild lactic-acid fermentation of fruit. Adds sour and funky notes. Used in modern cocktail purees.
Lillet Rose
Pink rose variant of Lillet. Used in modern Vesper-style cocktails.
Liqueur (vs spirit)
Spirit with added sugar (minimum 100g/L). Cointreau is a liqueur; gin is a spirit.

M

Maraschino liqueur
Cherry-pit liqueur from Croatia. Component of the Aviation, Last Word.
Mezcal
Smoky agave spirit from Mexico. Distinct from tequila. See mezcal vs tequila.
Milk wash
Clarification technique using milk to remove cloudiness. See milk-washing.
Mocktail / Zero-proof
Non-alcoholic cocktail. See the savoury mocktail manifesto.
Muddle
To press fruit, herbs, or sugar at the bottom of a shaker or glass to release flavour.
Madeira
Portuguese fortified wine, oxidatively aged. From dry to sweet.
Mai Tai (template)
Aged rum + lime + orange curaçao + orgeat. The 1944 Trader Vic original.
Martinez
The 1880s precursor to the Martini. Gin + sweet vermouth + maraschino + bitters.
Mash bill
The grain mixture used to make a whiskey. Bourbon is at least 51% corn; rye is at least 51% rye.
Mixology
The art and science of mixing drinks. Often used to mean "modern cocktail-making with technique-forward emphasis".
Modifier
A secondary ingredient (vermouth, amaro, liqueur) that shapes the spirit without dominating.
Mug (mule)
Copper mug used for Moscow Mule and other ginger-beer-based drinks. Insulates well.
Maraschino cherry
Brined cherry used as garnish. Luxardo is the cocktail-bar standard (dark, syrup-cured, never the red dye-bombs).
Mash bill
The grain composition of a whisky. Bourbon: minimum 51% corn. Rye: minimum 51% rye.
Milk punch (clarified)
The modern milk-wash technique. Spirit + citrus + dairy + spice; the dairy clarifies, you strain through coffee filter, get a translucent silky cocktail.
Mole bitters
Modern aromatic bitters with chilli and chocolate. Component of Mexican-inspired Old Fashioneds.
Muddler types
Wooden (traditional), nylon (durable), seedless (wide-tip for fragile herbs). Wooden absorbs flavour; nylon does not.

N

Nick & Nora glass
Small stemmed cocktail glass, smaller than a coupe. Used for spirit-forward cocktails.
Neat
Spirit served unmixed, room temperature, no ice, no glass chilling.
Negroni (template)
1:1:1 gin + Campari + sweet vermouth. The most influential bitter cocktail family.
Nikka
Major Japanese whisky producer. From The Barrel is a cocktail favourite.
NA aperitif
Non-alcoholic aperitif (Lyre's Italian Spritz, Martini Vibrante). For NA Negroni and NA Spritz.
Nutmeg
Warm aromatic spice, grated as a garnish over creamy cocktails (Brandy Alexander, Eggnog).
Nick & Nora glass
Small stemmed coupe-like glass, smaller than a coupe. Used for spirit-forward up cocktails.
Negroni Sbagliato
"Mistaken Negroni". Campari + sweet vermouth + prosecco. The bubbly variant.

O

Old Fashioned
Spirit + sugar + bitters + expressed peel. The original cocktail format.
Oleo saccharum
Citrus oil and sugar extract. See oleo saccharum.
Orgeat
Almond syrup with orange-flower-water notes. Component of the Mai Tai.
Old Tom gin
Slightly sweetened gin style, between London Dry and genever.
Olive (garnish)
Standard Martini garnish. Spanish manzanilla is the cocktail-bar default.
Orange flower water
Distillate from orange blossom. Component of Ramos Gin Fizz, Mai Tai.
Overproof rum
Rum bottled above 50% ABV. Wray & Nephew, Lemon Hart 151. Used in tiki floats.
Oleo-saccharum (lemon)
Citrus peel + sugar method that extracts citrus oils into the sugar. The base of punch. See oleo saccharum.
On the rocks
Served over ice (as opposed to "up" or "neat"). Old Fashioned, Negroni, Boulevardier all "on the rocks".
Osmanthus
Sweet-apricot-floral aromatic. Used in Chinese teas and in our house Stone Juniper cocktail.

P

Pandan
Southeast Asian leaf with perfumed-grassy flavour. See pandan in beverages.
Peychaud's bitters
New Orleans aniseed-citrus bitters. Component of the Sazerac.
Pisco
South American grape brandy. Pisco Sour is the canonical drink.
Prosecco
Italian sparkling wine. Lighter than champagne; component of the Spritz family.
Punch
Multi-ingredient batched format: spirit + citrus + sugar + water + spice. The grandfather of modern cocktails.
Paloma (template)
Tequila + grapefruit + lime + soda. Mexico's most popular tequila cocktail.
Pandan syrup
Sugar simmered with pandan leaves. The base of the Pandan Collins.
Peach bitters
Aromatic bitters with stone-fruit emphasis. Useful for brown-spirit builds.
Peg (Indian whisky measure)
South Asian whisky measure: small peg (30ml), large peg (60ml), patiala peg (around 120ml).
Pomelo
Largest citrus fruit, sweeter than grapefruit. See pomelo.
Plymouth gin
Protected geographical gin style from Plymouth, England. Softer and earthier than London Dry.
Pousse-café
Layered cocktail using density differences. Visual showpiece, hard to do at home.
Pina Colada
Rum + pineapple + coconut cream. 1954 Puerto Rico classic.
Phenols
The compound family responsible for smoke and medicinal flavours in Islay whisky. Source of "peat" character.
Pomace brandy
Brandy made from grape pomace (the leftover skins and seeds after wine pressing). Grappa is pomace brandy.
Pour count
The number of seconds of free pour the bartender uses to measure spirit. Standard four-count = 1 oz / 30ml.
Pre-batched cocktail
Drink mixed in advance and bottled for service. Common for parties and high-volume bars.

R

Reposado
Aged tequila (2 months to 1 year in oak). Between blanco and añejo.
Rich syrup
2:1 sugar-to-water syrup. Thicker and sweeter than simple (1:1). See simple syrup guide.
Rinse
Coating a glass with a small amount of strong spirit then discarding. Aromatic.
Robusta
Higher-caffeine, more bitter coffee species. Common in Malaysian kopi. See robusta for coffee cocktails.
Roselle
See Hibiscus.
Rambutan
Hairy red Malaysian fruit, lychee cousin. See rambutan.
Recipe ratio
The proportions in a classic cocktail (e.g., 2:1:1 spirit:citrus:sweet for a sour).
Reverse dry shake
Modern egg-white sour technique: shake with ice first, then dry shake to build foam. Some prefer this to traditional dry shake.
Rhum agricole
French Caribbean rum distilled from fresh sugarcane juice (not molasses). Grassy, vegetal, distinctive.
Rye whisky
American whisky made from at least 51% rye grain. Spicier than bourbon. Standard for Manhattan, Sazerac.
Rocks glass
Short, sturdy glass for spirits served over ice. Also called Old Fashioned glass or lowball.
Rosewater
Distillate from rose petals. Used in Persian / Indian / Middle Eastern cocktail-making.

S

Saline solution
20% salt-in-water dropper bottle. Amplifies flavour. See salt in cocktails.
SCOBY
Symbiotic Culture of Bacteria and Yeast. The kombucha mother.
Shake
To mix cocktail ingredients with ice in a sealed tin. Hard 10-12 seconds for sours. See how to shake.
Shrub
Vinegar-based fruit syrup, used as a cocktail mixer. See shrubs and switchels.
Simple syrup
1:1 sugar to water. The default sweetener.
Sour (cocktail family)
Spirit + citrus + sweetener in a balanced ratio. See the sour template.
Spritz
Wine-or-aperitivo + sparkling + ice + garnish in a wine glass. Aperol Spritz, Hugo, Negroni Sbagliato.
Stir
Mixing technique used for spirit-forward, no-citrus cocktails. Slower than shaking, more controlled dilution. See stirred vs shaken.
Saffron
The world's most expensive spice. Hay-honey-floral. See saffron.
Sake (junmai)
Japanese rice wine made without added alcohol. The standard cocktail sake.
Sazerac (template)
Rye + sugar + Peychaud's + absinthe rinse. The original cocktail (1838 New Orleans claim).
Scotch (blended)
Mixed Scotch whisky from multiple distilleries. Monkey Shoulder, Famous Grouse, Johnnie Walker Black.
Scotch (single malt)
Whisky from a single Scottish distillery, malted barley only.
Sherry (amontillado / oloroso / fino)
Spanish fortified wine in dry styles. Increasingly common in modern cocktails.
Smash (format)
Spirit + lemon + sugar + muddled mint + crushed ice. Whiskey Smash is the canonical version.
Star anise
Eight-pointed Chinese spice with intense liquorice. Winter cocktail aromatic. See star anise.
Suze
French gentian-based aperitif. The "modifier" in the White Negroni.
Swizzle
Caribbean technique using a swizzle stick spun between palms to chill and aerate.
Syrup (1:1, 2:1)
Sweetener concentration: 1:1 simple is standard; 2:1 rich is thicker and sweeter.
Sloe gin
Gin steeped with sloe berries (wild plum cousin). Dark red, sweet-tart. Used in Sloe Gin Fizz.
Sous-vide infusion
Modern technique using a precision-controlled water bath to infuse spirit with botanicals quickly and consistently.
Spent ice
The diluted partially-melted ice left in the shaker after straining. Should be tossed; never reused.
Speed rail
The metal rail at the bartender's hip holding the most-used bottles. The bar's working set.
Sugar inversion
Acid-catalysed breakdown of sucrose into glucose + fructose. Why old simple syrup tastes different from fresh.
Sherry cobbler
Pre-prohibition American format. Sherry + orange + sugar + crushed ice + berries.

T

Tepache
Mexican pineapple ferment. See tepache cocktails.
Tincture
Single-ingredient flavour extract in high-proof spirit. See tinctures and bitters.
Tiki
Mid-20th-century rum-and-tropical-fruit cocktail style. Mai Tai, Painkiller, Zombie.
Twist
Strip of citrus peel used as garnish. Usually expressed before placing.
Tamarind
Sweet-sour fruit pulp used in cocktails for molasses-depth tartness. See tamarind.
Tequila (blanco / reposado / añejo)
The three main tequila aging tiers. Blanco unaged, reposado 2 months-1 year, añejo 1-3 years.
Throw
Pouring liquid back and forth between two vessels held high. Aerates and chills without ice dilution.
Tom Collins (format)
Gin + lemon + sugar + soda in a tall glass. The original "sour with soda".
Tonic water
Quinine-bitter carbonated mixer. The "T" in G&T. Modern tonics (Fever-Tree, East Imperial) are less sweet than mainstream.
Tasting flight
Small samples of multiple drinks served together. Common for spirits (whisky flight, mezcal flight) and modern cocktail tasting menus.
Tiki bitters
Aromatic bitters with allspice, cinnamon, and cherry. Used in Painkiller and tiki classics.
Triple sec
Generic name for orange liqueur. Cointreau is the highest-grade triple sec. Cheaper triple sec is acceptable in volume builds.
Truffle (in cocktails)
Modern bartending: shaved truffle on Old Fashioned variants. Polarising.
Tuak
Sarawakian fermented rice wine. See tuak.

V

Vermouth
Fortified wine flavoured with botanicals. See vermouth explained.
Vanilla
Sweet floral seed pod from a Mexican orchid. See vanilla.
Vesper
The James Bond cocktail. Gin + vodka + Lillet Blanc. From Casino Royale (1953).
Volume vs ratio
Recipe expression conventions. Volume = "60ml gin"; ratio = "2 parts gin". Pros use both.
Vacuum infusion
Modern technique using a vacuum chamber to force flavour from solid ingredient into liquid quickly. The bartender's centrifuge alternative.
VSOP / XO Cognac
Cognac age classifications. VSOP minimum 4 years; XO minimum 10 years.
Vermouth (storage)
Vermouth is fortified wine; it oxidises after opening. Refrigerate after opening; use within 4 weeks for cocktail-grade flavour.

Y

Yuzu
Japanese citrus, aromatic-bright, more floral than lemon. Used in the Junos Sour.
Yeast (wild)
Natural-environment yeasts used in spontaneous fermentation. See wild fermentation.

W

Whisk(e)y
Grain-based spirit. "Whisky" in Scotland, Japan, Canada; "whiskey" in Ireland, USA. See whisky vs whiskey.
Whiskey Sour (format)
Bourbon + lemon + sugar + (egg white). The American sour-template archetype.
White vermouth
See Bianco vermouth (Italian) or dry vermouth (French).
Wine glass
Used for spritz-format cocktails (Aperol Spritz, Hugo Spritz).
Wormwood
The bitter herb in absinthe and vermouth. Source of the historical "thujone" controversy.
Water (cocktail-grade)
Filtered or distilled water. Tap water with chlorine taints clear-spirit cocktails.
Wash (fat-, milk-, oil-)
Infusion techniques where you mix spirit with a fat, then separate the fat to leave flavour behind. See fat washing.
White Negroni
Gin + Suze + Lillet Blanc. 2001 modern classic.

X

XO Cognac
"Extra Old" classification for cognac, minimum 10 years aged.

Z

Zest
The outer coloured layer of citrus peel. Where the aromatic oils live.
Zero-proof
See Mocktail.
Zoom level (citrus)
Informal bartender term for how aggressively you express a citrus peel. "Heavy zoom" = strong oil expression.
