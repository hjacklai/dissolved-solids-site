Cili padi in cocktails.

How much is too much, when to muddle vs infuse, what pairs with the heat, and the small-chilli mistakes we've made so you don't have to. Three drinks where it works.

6 min read·Notes from Dissolved Solids

Cili padi, the small Malaysian bird's eye chilli, is one of the most concentrated heat sources you'll meet in any kitchen. Half a single fruit, muddled into a drink, will register on the tongue for ten minutes. A whole fruit, the same way, will close down your palate for the rest of the evening. The line between "good idea" and "ruined the drink" is narrower with cili padi than with almost any other ingredient we use.

What cili padi actually is

Capsicum frutescens, the bird's eye chilli. Roughly 50,000-100,000 Scoville heat units, which puts it in the same range as a Thai chilli or a Mexican piquín. For comparison: jalapeño is 2,500-8,000 SHU. Cili padi is roughly fifteen times hotter than the chilli you've seen in a margarita on a Mexican menu.

The capsaicin in cili padi is concentrated in the white pith and the seeds, not in the flesh of the fruit. If you split a cili padi lengthwise and remove the seeds and pith, you can use just the green flesh for a green-pepper flavour without much heat. If you leave them in, the heat takes over.

The dosing scale we use

Per drink:

  • No heat: one cili padi, split lengthwise, seeds and pith removed, flesh only, muddled into the drink. You get green pepper and tropical fruit notes without the burn.
  • Whisper: half a cili padi (split lengthwise, seeds intact), muddled lightly with the sweetener. The heat is there if you look for it, gone if you don't.
  • Warm: half a cili padi, seeds intact, muddled with sweetener and citrus. Slow heat that builds across the drink.
  • Genuinely hot: a whole cili padi, seeds intact, muddled. The drink reads as "this is a chilli cocktail" rather than "this cocktail has chilli in it."
  • Don't: two whole cili padi in one drink. We've done it. Once.

For the cili padi margarita on our builder, we use the "warm" dose by default . half a fruit, seeds in, muddled with the agave syrup before the shake.

Muddle vs infuse

Two main ways to get cili padi heat into a drink:

Muddle. Crush the fruit into the syrup or the base of the shaker before adding everything else. This gives an immediate, brighter heat that comes out fast and fades fast. Good for shaken drinks served immediately. The downside is that the heat is concentrated in the first few sips and softens over the drink's life as ice dilutes.

Infuse. Steep cili padi in alcohol for a defined period . usually 12 to 48 hours in a neutral spirit like vodka or tequila. The chilli oil leaches into the spirit. Strain. You now have a chilli-infused spirit you can dose precisely. We do this when we want predictable heat across many drinks. The downside is that infusions are intense; an infused spirit will be much hotter per millilitre than the original fruit, so dose very small amounts.

A useful trick: infuse for a known time, then strain into separate small bottles labelled by intensity. We keep a "low," "medium," and "hot" infused tequila in the back, dosed by drops not millilitres.

What pairs with cili padi heat

Heat is a partner ingredient, not a solo. It needs something to play against.

  • Sweetness tames the heat almost completely. Agave syrup, gula melaka, honey. The chilli reads as warmth rather than burn.
  • Citrus brightens the heat. Lime works especially well; calamansi works best of all.
  • Tropical fruit (mango, pineapple, passionfruit) carries chilli beautifully. The fruit cools the perception of heat while the heat sharpens the fruit.
  • Tequila and mezcal are the natural base spirits. Gin works if it's juniper-forward. Vodka works for cleaner profiles.
  • Ginger stacks well with chilli . two heat sources, different positions on the tongue.

What doesn't pair: cream-based drinks, coffee drinks, anything where the dominant note is bitter. The chilli either gets lost or fights the existing flavour.

Three drinks where it works

Cili padi margarita. Tequila, lime, agave, half a cili padi muddled, smoked salt rim. Our default. We have one on the builder.

Pineapple-chilli highball. Vodka, fresh pineapple juice, half a cili padi muddled with palm sugar syrup, top with chilled soda. Easy summer drink. Goes with grilled food.

Cili padi negroni. A controversial one. Gin, Campari, sweet vermouth, plus a single dropper-dispensed drop of cili padi tincture. The bitter and the heat play in interesting ways. We don't make this often, but when we do, people remember it.

What we've learned from getting it wrong

The mistake we've made most: assuming cili padi will fade across the drink the way lime juice does. It doesn't. Capsaicin is fat-soluble, not water-soluble, so it doesn't dilute meaningfully as the ice melts. A drink that starts at "warm" stays at "warm" all the way down.

This is also why milk-based drinks would, in theory, mute chilli effectively. We haven't found a great recipe that combines them yet. If you have, tell us.

One small kitchen safety note

If you handle cili padi at home, the capsaicin gets onto your fingers and stays there. Don't rub your eyes for the rest of the evening. Wash with soap and oil, not just water (water doesn't dissolve capsaicin, which is the same reason drinking water doesn't help with the burn . milk or yogurt is what actually neutralises it on the palate).

Cili padi rewards restraint. The drinks where it works are the ones where it's a partner ingredient, not the lead. If a guest asks for "extra chilli" we usually start with the standard dose first and add more incrementally. We've never had anyone walk away wishing it were milder.

Frequently asked questions

What is cili padi?

Cili padi (Capsicum frutescens, bird's eye chilli) is the small Malaysian chilli that ranks 50,000 to 100,000 Scoville heat units, roughly fifteen times hotter than a jalapeno. The capsaicin is concentrated in the white pith and seeds, not the green flesh. Split, deseeded, and pithed, cili padi gives green-pepper flavour without much heat; left whole it dominates.

How much cili padi should I put in a cocktail?

Half a fruit with seeds intact, muddled with the sweetener, is the working warm dose for one drink (margarita, highball). For a whisper of heat, half a fruit muddled lightly. For green pepper only and no burn, one fruit deseeded and depithed, flesh only. A whole cili padi turns the drink into a chilli cocktail rather than a cocktail with chilli. Two is a mistake we have made once.

Should I muddle or infuse cili padi?

Muddle for shaken drinks served immediately; you get bright, immediate heat that fades faster across the drink. Infuse cili padi in vodka or tequila for 12 to 48 hours when you want precise, predictable dosing across many drinks. Infusions concentrate the heat and should be dosed by drops not millilitres. Bartenders often keep low, medium, and hot infused tequila bottled separately for service.
