Cocktail ingredients.

A working encyclopedia of the ingredients we use behind the bar, with focus on Malaysian botanicals you will not find on Western cocktail sites. Each entry covers what it is, where to source it locally, how to prep it, and which cocktails it builds.

Updated May 2026·50 ingredients and growing

Most cocktail-ingredient references are Western-default. Juniper, mint, lemon, lime. The Malaysian palate carries pandan, gula melaka, calamansi, kopi-O, kaffir lime leaf, asam boi, bunga kantan, hibiscus, lemongrass, butterfly pea, cili padi, tamarind. These ingredients do real work in cocktails; most international cocktail sites barely cover them. This encyclopedia is our attempt to fill that gap, alongside the global classics every bar relies on.

How to use this encyclopedia

Each ingredient page covers the same six questions:

  1. What it is. Botanical or product family. Where it comes from.
  2. Flavour profile. What it tastes like, how it presents in a drink.
  3. Where to source it in Malaysia. Wet markets, supermarkets, specialty suppliers. Notes on quality grades.
  4. How to prep it. Syrup, infusion, fresh juice, garnish, dehydrated, smoked, fat-washed, whatever the standard moves are.
  5. Best cocktail pairings. Three to six classic uses, linked to recipes in our database.
  6. Substitutions. What to use if you cannot source the real thing.
