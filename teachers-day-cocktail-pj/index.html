Teachers' Day in PJ.

Planning a Teachers' Day visit (16 May, Hari Guru) in Petaling Jaya. Taking a former teacher (Cikgu, Sir, Madam) out for a drink, the small-bar advantage for quiet conversation, our non-alcoholic side, and how to make the visit feel considered rather than performative.

Hari Guru (16 May) is the Malaysian Teachers' Day. The cultural format is school-led: students bring cards, gifts, sometimes flowers. As adults, the small-scale version is "I should take a teacher I am still in touch with out for a drink or a coffee". Here is how to do that properly at a cocktail bar in Petaling Jaya.

Teachers' Day in the Malaysian context

Hari Guru commemorates the 1956 adoption of the Razak Report, which set the foundation for the modern Malaysian education system. It is observed across all schools (national, vernacular, private, tuition centres) with morning assemblies and student-led performances. It is not a federal public holiday; schools and the rest of the country operate on a normal day.

The adult-life version of Hari Guru is the calendar excuse to reconnect with a former teacher, lecturer, or mentor. In PJ specifically:

  • The morning belongs to current students and current schools.
  • Many KL teachers commute back to PJ in the evening; the after-school window from 5pm onwards is when the adult-visit format works.
  • Group bookings tend toward 2 to 4 people (former student plus teacher, sometimes a partner or fellow alumnus). Larger reunion groups of 4 to 12 also happen if a cohort gathers around a beloved teacher.
  • The Cikgu/Sir/Madam respect angle matters: the bar choice should feel considered, not flashy.
  • Many Malaysian teachers do not drink alcohol; the NA programme has to be real for the visit to work.

This is the most under-served bar occasion of the year. Most teachers do not get invited out for a drink on Hari Guru. The ones who do, land well.

The Teachers' Day adult plan

If you are an adult and there is a teacher (or former teacher, lecturer, mentor) who still matters to you, 16 May is the right calendar excuse to send a message. The format that works:

  • Message a week ahead: "Cikgu, it's Hari Guru next week. Are you free for a coffee or a drink on the weekend?"
  • Pick the right venue. A small bar with a proper coffee programme works whether the teacher drinks or not.
  • Pay for the visit. The point is to thank them.
  • Plan 90 minutes. Long enough for real conversation, short enough that neither party feels obligated to stretch it.

What to order if your teacher drinks

For teachers who appreciate a properly-built drink. Default to recognisable classics rather than statement experiments.

Negroni. Bitter, herbal, considered. Reads as an adult drink. Pairs with the kind of conversation you have with someone who taught you something that stuck. Working guide.

Old Fashioned. Spirit-forward, slow-evolving. The drink rewards patience, which matches the visit format. Working guide.

White Negroni or Vesper. For teachers who prefer something less aggressively bitter than a standard Negroni.

Pandan Collins. Malaysian-local, gentle, recognisable. Most teachers will identify the pandan note from kuih. Recipe.

A glass of single-malt Scotch or aged rum, neat. The simplest possible expression of "I want to share something good with you".

The evening plan

16 May falls on different weekdays each year. Most teachers are at school during the day on a weekday Teachers' Day. The realistic time slot is:

  • Weekday Teachers' Day: evening visit, 6pm to 7:30pm.
  • Weekend Teachers' Day (16 May falls on Sat or Sun): afternoon visit, 3pm to 5pm.

If 16 May falls mid-week and the teacher is genuinely busy, plan the visit for the weekend before or after. The date is the calendar excuse, not the hard requirement.

For Muslim teachers

Many of our teachers are Muslim and the cocktail bar might feel unfamiliar. A few honest things:

  • The non-alcoholic side is real. Glassware matches; bartender attention is equal.
  • The room is small and quiet during the Sunday or weekday afternoon visit.
  • If you brief us when you book, we will set up an NA-first build sheet for the visit so the bartender does not lead with alcoholic suggestions.
  • Outside food is welcome (some Muslim guests prefer to bring their own snacks from a halal kopitiam nearby; we are fine with that).

How to thank a teacher without it feeling like a transaction

The thank-you that lands best is usually the simplest: "You taught me X, and I still think about it. I wanted to take you out today and tell you that." Then order, sit, drink, talk. The visit is the gift; you do not need a wrapped present on top of it.

If you do want to bring something physical, the easiest move is a small bag of good coffee beans (we can recommend a local roaster) or a tin of loose-leaf tea. Practical, not ostentatious.
